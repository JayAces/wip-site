Section 0 · Standing Rule

Every Number in This Document Is Traceable

Read before using any figure from this blueprint

All figures here were recomputed on 6 September 2026 directly from the Crisis Tracker export and carry their denominators. The authoritative list lives in wip-source-of-truth-figures.md in the Strategic Communications project. If a number does not appear there, it does not go in front of anyone.

Retired and no longer usable: 115+ Warriors, 145+ Warriors, 83%, 6.8, 23% menstrual trigger, 4x admission gap, 33% protocol non-adherence, 63 days to findings, the 23-hour onset-to-care average. Each has a replacement in the source-of-truth document.

Submissions are not Warriors. 145 crisis submissions came from 113 Warriors. The published JSCD abstract already draws this distinction, reporting 87 reports from 77 participants for its window. Never use the two words interchangeably.

The numbers move. This is live data. Geography is derived from the hospital a Warrior names plus the area code of the number they give for check-ins, because submissions are anonymous. Refreshing these figures is a standing job, not a one-time fix.

Section I · The Lead Magnet

The Warrior ER Survival Guide

A data-grounded, community-owned field guide to navigating the system, built from actual WIP submissions. The only guide of its kind, because the only people who could write it are Warriors.

Why this asset, and why only WIP can make it

Every other SCD resource on the internet is written by institutions, for institutions. The Warrior ER Survival Guide draws from 145 anonymous crisis submissions logged by 113 Warriors to produce findings no clinical team has published, because no clinical team was in the room when Warriors were living it.

This isn't a generic wellness download. It's intelligence that was community-produced and community-owned, and the guide makes that origin story explicit on every page. The draw IS the proof of concept.

2× as likely to report treatment was working when admitted, 57% against 28% 35 admitted / 29 released
70°F 7 in 10 crisis reports came from below this, median 57°F 132 reporting temperature

Credibility Stack

The guide does not stand alone. It sits behind a peer-reviewed publication, Journal of Sickle Cell Disease, Vol. 3 Suppl. 1, June 2026, and a bylined feature in Challenger Community News from the same week. Institutional audiences meet the citation first. Community audiences meet the guide first. Both doors lead to the tracker.

Gating Strategy

Full gate, email plus first name only. Every extra field reduces conversion 5 to 10%. Deliver on the thank-you page immediately and by email as backup, so nobody in crisis is waiting on a confirmation click. Thank-you page carries one next step: "Log your first crisis and add your data to the next edition."

Privacy line under the form, every time: "Your email is for the guide and optional WIP updates. It is never added to a crisis record or shared with hospitals, insurers, or employers."

A practical ER companion usable in minutes, with deeper context for when there is time. PDF plus web-readable, designed for mobile.

Section II · The Pipeline

The Unreasonable Hospitality Funnel

Healthcare processes Warriors. WIP receives them. Crisis logging is not the ask. It's the natural destination for someone who arrived feeling seen.

"In healthcare, 'hospitality' has been stripped down to efficiency. Unreasonable hospitality means giving more than a Warrior expects before asking for anything in return, and then asking once, clearly, with full explanation of why it matters." KCH Framework · Hii Layer Integration

The pipeline, step by step

Arrival

Discovery

Organic social, ShinePR earned media, the Challenger feature, the JSCD citation, partner referrals (SCWB, CBOs), Reddit and SCD communities, word of mouth from Warriors.

The Draw

Warrior ER Survival Guide

Landing page with a clear value exchange. Email plus optional first name. Feels like receiving a gift, not entering a database. Immediate delivery, no confirmation gate.

The Receive

Welcome sequence, 3 emails over 7 days

Pure hospitality. Two emails give before the third asks. Warriors learn what WIP knows. They see their own experience reflected in the data.

The Invitation

Log Your First Crisis

Not a cold CTA, a warm continuation. "You've seen what 113 Warriors built. Add your experience to the next edition." Under 3 minutes. Anonymous. A contribution, not a transaction.

Longitudinal

Warrior Community Member

Warrior ID established. Submission history builds. Quarterly data briefs land in their inbox showing what the community found. They become the asset they downloaded.

Welcome email sequence

Email 01 · Day 0 · Immediate delivery
"Your ER Guide is here. Start with this page."

Deliver the guide and point straight at the toolkit in Section 04, so a Warrior in a waiting room finds the scripts without reading the theory first. Say plainly that WIP is community-owned and that this email list is separate from crisis submissions. Tone: receiving, not selling.

Email 02 · Day 3 · Pure value, no ask
"A pattern 21% of Warriors told us to take seriously."

Deep dive on the menstrual cycle finding. What Warriors reported, with the denominator shown. Why standard intake doesn't ask. Frame it honestly: not the biggest trigger in the data, the one nobody is looking for. No CTA to log. Just: here's what your community already knows. You're part of this now.

Email 03 · Day 7 · Soft invitation
"If you choose to add your experience, here's what happens next."

Introduce the tracker as authorship, not data entry. Explain anonymity, ownership, what is collected, and what it will never be used for, before asking for anything. Avoid "your crisis is intelligence" as the opener: it's mission-true but reads as extractive to someone whose crisis may be active right now.

Consent Architecture Is Part of the Product

This audience has every reason to be wary of institutions collecting their data. Keep the guide download and future WIP contact visibly separate, explain exactly what future email will contain, and make unsubscribe obvious. The trust you spend here is the trust you need at the tracker.

Design Principle for the Evolution

Data stays front, warmth is the surround. The crisis logging CTA remains the dominant action on the page. Every new section is additive: it wraps the existing architecture in human context without burying it. A Warrior on a phone in crisis should still be able to reach "Log Your Crisis" in two taps. Everything else is for the Warrior who has three minutes and needs to feel received.

Publishing Discipline

The site source now lives in a git repository on the working machine, and the deploy runs from that repository. Never edit the live site through a host interface: if it happens, pull the served HTML back into the repo and commit it before making further changes, or the next deploy silently reverts it. That failure mode is exactly how the repo, the local folder, and the live site drifted into three different versions.

The Bottom Line

The Warrior ER Survival Guide is not a side project. It is the proof that WIP can generate institutional-grade intelligence AND deliver it back to the community as a tangible gift. That dual motion, intelligence in and value out, is the engine of every phase that follows. It is also the cleanest possible argument for the 501(c)(3): here is what community-owned data infrastructure produces when it is operated with unreasonable hospitality.

The discipline added in September 2026 is part of that argument, not a tax on it. Every figure traceable, every quote verbatim, every denominator shown. An organization that corrects its own numbers before anyone else catches them is exactly the kind of organization institutions can license data from.
